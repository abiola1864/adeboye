What Democratic Accountability Requires Beyond Elections

Kunle J. Adeboye · May 2, 2026 · 6 min read
Elections are a necessary condition for democracy, but they are not sufficient. Accountability requires much more than the right to vote.

Every four years, Nigeria holds elections. Citizens queue, cast ballots, and wait for results. Yet something essential is missing from this picture: accountability. Democratic accountability is not just about choosing leaders — it is about maintaining the ability to hold them responsible throughout their time in office.

The Accountability Gap

In a functioning democracy, elections are the most visible accountability mechanism, but far from the only one. Between elections, citizens rely on a network of institutions — a free press, independent judiciary, active legislature, civil society organizations, and access to information — to keep government in check.

Nigeria has many of these institutions in formal terms. The challenge is that they frequently fail to perform their accountability functions in practice.

What Real Accountability Looks Like

Countries with strong democratic cultures do not simply hold elections. They maintain active oversight through parliamentary committees that genuinely scrutinize executive action, investigative journalism with legal protection and financial sustainability, access to information laws that are enforced, not just enacted, and an active citizenry that engages between election cycles.

“Accountability without enforcement is theatre. Democracies require both the formal mechanisms and the political culture to use them.”

Building the Accountability Infrastructure

Nigeria’s democratic deficit is not primarily electoral — it is the weakness of the systems that should operate between elections. Strengthening these systems requires investment: in press freedom, judicial independence, legislative capacity, and most importantly, a civic culture that demands accountability as a right, not a favour.

Democracy is not an event. It is a practice, sustained daily by institutions, norms, and citizens who refuse to accept less than they are owed.
